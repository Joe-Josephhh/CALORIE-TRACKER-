1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & KEY FINDINGS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This investigation evaluated the personalized LSTM weight prediction engine
against a strict, non-leakage chronological holdout across users stratified
by their logged history length (Group A: <20d, B: 20-29d, C: 30-40d, D: >40d).
Each user's predictions were compared head-to-head against a Naive Persistence
Baseline (Predicted next-day weight = latest observed weight at prediction time).

Key takeaways from the empirical evaluation:
  - Eligibility Boundary: Users with <14 days cannot run the LSTM, correctly
    enforced by the production threshold (e.g. User_042 with 3 days was excluded).
  - Persistence Baseline Strength: In short or intermittent histories (Group B),
    the persistence baseline is extremely tough to beat because daily weight swings
    are small and forward-filled gaps artificially reward a zero-delta prediction.
  - Where LSTM Wins: For users with genuine daily caloric deficits and steady
    downward trends (e.g., User_043), the LSTM successfully anticipates weight drops
    from energy deficits and outperforms the lagging persistence baseline.
  - Long-Term Stability (Group D): With 139+ training sequences, the LSTM achieves
    exceptional absolute accuracy (~0.071 kg MAE / 0.090 kg RMSE), matching the baseline
    without runaway divergence thanks to the physiological tanh dampening.

4. METHODOLOGICAL ASSUMPTIONS & LIMITATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Small Sample Uncertainty: User counts per group are small (1 to 3 users). Estimates
    have wide confidence intervals and should be interpreted as diagnostic indicators
    rather than population-level certainties.
  - Gap Imputation Artifacts: Users with multi-week logging gaps (such as User_040
    and User_041 who logged 20 days then had a 21-day hiatus) have forward-filled weights
    during the gap. This flatlines true variance and gives an artificial advantage
    to the persistence baseline during those intervals.
  - Strict Leakage Prevention: Normalization scalers were fitted strictly on [0..cutoff],
    preventing lookahead bias. Validation sequences were evaluated in natural time order.
